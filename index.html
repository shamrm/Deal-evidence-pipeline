Proof of concept · Private real estate · Cedar Point Tower, Business Bay

From scattered deal documents to numbers you can trust

Every document is filed once, unchanged, in a central store. Every number taken from it points back to its exact source and date. Code checks the AI's work, the machine approves only what it can prove, and a person decides the rest.

The problemDeal facts are spread across memos, decks, spreadsheets, emails and calls.
The goalCapture it into one place, structure it, and flag missing, inconsistent or outdated data.
The hard partHow to automate with AI without ending up with numbers nobody can trust.

The data structure

Two stores, one link between them

Files live in an object store (S3 on AWS; SeaweedFS locally, same code). Facts live in a database. The highlighted parts are what the selected step uses.

Evidence store · bucket deal-lake

The archive room: files, never edited
Incoming sources Adviser emails, data room, uploads, Teams calls. Read once at capture, never again.
raw/{document_id}/ The original, exactly as received, plus its stamp. 2026-10-15_occupancy_update.eml
meta.json (sender, received, SHA-256, channel)
parsed/{document_id}.json The text read from it, labelled by page, slide, sheet or speaker.
extracted/{document_id}.json The AI's answer exactly as returned, kept for audit.

Rule: raw/ is evidence. The other two folders are working copies that can always be rebuilt from it.

Facts store · Postgres

The index cards and the logbook
dealsWhich investments we track
field_catalogAgreed data points: occupancy, NOI, risks… with unit and valid range
documentsEvery file received: who sent it, when, its fingerprint, type and dealdeal_id → deals · s3_key → raw/ file · parent → its email
factsOne value each: as-of date, exact quote and location, method (code/AI/person), confidence, statusdocument_id → documents · field_key → field_catalog
flagsMissing, inconsistent, outdatedfact_ids → facts
audit_logEvery decision: who, what, why

Rule: facts are never overwritten. A correction adds a new fact and marks the old one superseded.

factdocument_id→ documents3_key→ original file in the store

Why dates matter

Different numbers are not always a conflict

Each fact records the date it was true. Three occupancy figures from four documents turn out to be a decline over time, which is useful information in itself.

Occupancy, as reported

How the rules read it

92% · information memo and investor deck, as of 30 June
88% · rent roll (calculated by code), broker email and Teams call, as of 30 September
85% · broker email: a tenant moved out early, 15 October

Same date, different values: conflict, flagged.
Different dates: a trend. The newest value is current, and a steady decline is flagged for attention.

Automation and quality

The machine approves what it can prove

A fact is approved automatically only when confidence is at least 0.90 and no problem involves it. Risks and assumptions are always confirmed by a person, because code cannot check them.
